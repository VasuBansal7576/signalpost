# Exact-entity external discovery

The first benchmark only published facts from registry-listed domains that repeated the legal identity. Just seven of its 100 entities had a registry website. The same ownership rule was also incorrectly applied to recruitment platforms, and normal article extraction omitted many social-account links. A partial row means some requested fields remain unresolved; it does not mean the financial/registry collection failed.

The revised runner performs three distinct searches for website/contact information, employer recruitment and exact-organisation directory corroboration. Website search excludes business directories; hiring search includes public company careers sites without requiring prior website verification. Queries retain the quoted legal brand and request Norway-localized search ranking. Candidate inspection prioritizes exact legal identifiers, registered addresses, registry email domains and Norwegian context before URL length. Late identity/profile searches pass newly observed company candidates through the same contact-page inspection as early searches; candidate status never establishes ownership by itself. The identity search retains directories for corroborating links. It requests fresh full-page text and extracted links in the search response, preserves them in the job's source store and reuses that response for later reads. Provider-relative, protocol-relative and Markdown links are resolved against the observed page instead of being discarded. Non-HTTPS observed links are upgraded only to the same HTTPS host/path; reads still pass public-URL and attribution checks. It does not use titles or highlights as proof. This follows Exa's [Search content options](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/search-api-guide-for-coding-agents) and [Contents guidance](https://exa.ai/docs/contents/quickstart).

Website attribution supports registry links, exact organisation numbers on matching brand domains, legal-name/address/brand matches and corroborated exact-org directory links. Directories cannot promote one another into official websites. Recognized sector publishers and member/directory URL paths remain third-party, even when a generic industry or city word overlaps the domain. Freshly found exact-organisation directory records may supply observed brand-domain candidates, which are read and checked separately. They never publish the directory itself as the company site. An explicit conflicting organisation number is rejected. A team page may also be corroborated by the exact legal name, matching brand domain, Norwegian context and at least two distinct current registry officers. Registry-role evidence is preserved. One shared director or a customer page is insufficient. A legal contact page may support a branded homepage that omits the full legal name; a branch/operator page cannot establish ownership of a whole chain. A homepage naming the operator only in a later location list also needs its own exact legal ID or a verified legal-contact anchor; a shared telephone alone does not establish the operator homepage. Parent and franchise identity remains separate.

Hiring has its own employer gate. Public recruitment pages can qualify without being company-owned, provided the legal employer/brand and organisation or registered/workplace location agree. The quote must bind the role/policy to that employer. Explicitly expired ads and past application deadlines are rejected. A navigation button alone is insufficient. Supported observations distinguish an explicit no-open-roles statement, open-application policy, dated vacancy and recruitment information with no established active count. Group boilerplate is excluded from the published branch hiring value.

Company profiles are actual social-account URLs supported by a company-controlled outbound link. Verified-site HTML navigation is fetched with robots checks, pinned public DNS, TLS validation, response limits and same-host redirect limits when article extraction misses these links. The social platforms themselves are not fetched. Matching accepts legal brand spellings, URL transliterations and a corroborated primary website brand; a branch page cannot use that shortcut to inherit a parent's account. Personal profiles, sharing URLs and mismatching parent accounts are excluded. Single-quoted links and Norwegian URL transliterations are supported. Harmless social tracking parameters and trailing slashes are removed without changing the account identity; the original publisher link remains in the exact evidence quote.

Raw HTML is retained separately. Navigation excludes runtime/script URLs, while bounded valid JSON-LD retains legal identity and genuine company links. Raw HTML cannot directly support a published model claim. Verified company pages outrank unrelated recruitment results in the bounded model context. HTML extraction also preserves bounded description metadata as a separate source section. An exact description can survive model failure only on an attributed homepage/about page when its text names the company or the page itself contains the exact legal identifier; privacy and cookie text is excluded.

The verifier persists accepted proposals and returns a small receipt to the model. It does not repeatedly send the evidence bundle back into context. The single repair turn handles text-alignment errors using at most four small source windows; it does not attempt to rewrite wrong-company evidence into an accepted claim. The model context budget is 40,000 tokens; cumulative input across reasoning steps is separately bounded at 160,000. Completed model steps are metered as they arrive, including when a later stream times out; duplicate stream events are not counted twice.

A fresh job ID requests fresh web sources. The frozen registry remains the supplied identity anchor, and no bundled smoke outputs answer a new batch. The final comparison reports supported company counts, not invented active-job counts. Rows stay partial when an external field genuinely remains unresolved.

Final review found sector-directory false positives caused by a generic word (“borettslag”) being treated as a domain brand. Generic industry/legal words no longer establish brand ownership, and four claims from those sources were withdrawn. This validation ran against every preserved live company result.

The previous NAV website-search probe was rate-limited and excluded from the earlier v5 benchmark. It has been replaced by a complete official feed bootstrap, hydration of every active head, cursor catch-up and exact legal/subunit-ID queries for all 100 companies. The source excludes Finn and records unattributed employer IDs; it cannot establish company-wide absence. See [reliable fallbacks](RELIABLE_FALLBACKS.md) and the current [benchmark](BENCHMARK.md).

A model-copied job UUID typo during the v6 collection exposed a tool-binding defect. Tools now derive the job from the server-side Eve session receipt. An inspected terminal schema failure received one explicit corrective session; persisted successful receipts and uncertain billed calls are not blindly repeated. Original collection and follow-up measurements are retained separately.

Unpaid registry GETs retry one transient timeout, transport error or rate/server failure. Both physical attempts remain in the request ledger; permanent absence and billable web calls are not replayed. Optional controller discovery reserves 90 seconds plus the next request allowance for model extraction. The initial model view is eight deduplicated 1,600-character source excerpts, prioritizing actual recruitment/about/news content over legal boilerplate; full preserved sources remain locally inspectable. The cumulative 160K session guardrail remains unchanged. Registry-listed site candidates can use robots-approved HTML when Exa returns no readable page, without assuming ownership.

## Connected-source boundaries

The optional connected Exa parser separates every fetched publisher at its explicit URL wrapper, including responses with Author/Published metadata. It rejects unrequested sources, duplicate headers and unrecognized URL-wrapper formatting. A namesake page cannot inherit an organisation number from the next company-directory page. The regression test includes the complete attribution path; a replay of the failing real receipt now rejects the foreign website. The interrupted v17 run is diagnostic and excluded from the improvement figures.

This company-HTML transport excludes PDF and office-document downloads. Document redirects are discarded before receipt storage and cannot support claims. Official registry financial JSON and annual-report link metadata remain available; this restriction does not claim PDF extraction. Usable HTML receipts retain their original tool text.

Public activity must identify the requested entity in the published event text itself. A shared group footer cannot make a parent-company event into a subsidiary event. Rejected model proposals remain in the error ledger and are not published.
